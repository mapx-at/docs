DTM terrain model

The DTM is an elevation tile set for all of Austria: ground height without buildings and vegetation, encoded as color (Terrain-RGB). It works directly as raster-dem for hillshading and 3D terrain in MapLibre, Mapbox GL and compatible clients.

Use cases

Hiking and outdoor mapsHillshade as a map background up to z16
3D terrainTerrain and flight views with orthophotoElevation profilesAscent and descent along a route
Slope and aspect analysisGradient and orientation from the elevation values
QGIS and analysisElevation as raster in desktop GIS

Live map

Section titled “Live map”
Click the map: elevation and tile RGB.

Clicking reads the tile of the current zoom level (z16 at most) via range request, decodes the pixel at the click point with the formula under “Encoding” and shows elevation and RGB value. With 3D enabled, the value of queryTerrainElevation is shown as well.

Add to your map

Section titled “Add to your map”
  • MapLibre
  • Leaflet
  • OpenLayers
  • QGIS
  • Python
  • curl
  • JS / Rust

Leaflet has no raster-dem. The XYZ endpoint (API) delivers the raw tiles; they show the encoded color, not a map. For elevation values, decode the pixels yourself (see “Encoding”).

  1. In the Browser panel, right-click XYZ Tiles, New Connection.
  2. URL https://tiles.mapx.at/v1/dtm/{z}/{x}/{y}.webp, Min. Zoom 5, Max. Zoom 16, tile resolution 512.
  3. The layer shows the raw tiles (encoded color). Elevations follow from the formula in “Encoding” ((R*65536 + G*256 + B)*0.1 - 10000), for example in the Raster Calculator on the three bands.

Encoding

Section titled “Encoding”
  • Theoretical value range −10000 m to +1,667,721.5 m in steps of 0.1 m; in Austria the values lie between about 115 m (Lake Neusiedl) and about 3,798 m (Grossglockner).
  • Values are rounded to 0.1 m when written (round half to even at exactly .5, see Rust code above); values outside the range are clamped.
  • WebP lossless: lossy compression would directly corrupt the encoded elevations. Do not use alpha information; read the RGB bands unchanged (no color management, no premultiplied alpha).
  • 512 × 512 pixels, no border (no 1 px skirt). In MapLibre specify tileSize: 512 and encoding: "mapbox".

In detail: Terrain-RGB.

Vertical resolution and accuracy

Section titled “Vertical resolution and accuracy”
Value Meaning
Vertical resolution 0.1 m smallest height step of the encoding
Deviation from source ≤ 0.05 m rounding during encoding, otherwise unchanged (lossless)
Vertical accuracy of the source ± 0.50 m stated by BEV for the ALS-DTM; larger deviations are possible in high mountains
Height reference EVRF2000 Austria orthometric heights (EPSG:9274), as in the source

The encoding is five times finer than the accuracy of the survey, so it does not limit quality.

In the overview levels z15 to z5 each pixel is the mean of the finer level. The vertical resolution stays 0.1 m, but small peaks and summits are smoothed. For exact point heights read z16 or use the elevation API.

The data come from a 1 m raster. z16 (about 0.8 m per pixel) is the finest level and preserves the resolution of the source. There are no tiles above z16: from z17 MapLibre scales up the z16 tile (set maxzoom: 16). Levels z15 to z5 are averaged in elevation space (decode, average, re-encode), not resized as images.

Source and currency

Section titled “Source and currency”

The DTM is derived from the BEV ALS-DTM 1 m (airborne laser scanning, terrain only). The reference date is in the specifications and the file name; a file never changes, a new release is a new file.

  • Source: BEV (Austrian Federal Office of Metrology and Surveying), reference date 15 Sep 2025.
  • The source is in EPSG:3035 and is resampled bilinearly to Web Mercator, which avoids overshoot on steep cliffs.
  • Overview levels z15 to z5 are averaged in elevation space.

Notes

Section titled “Notes”
  • Quantization 0.1 m. The deviation from the source is within the rounding range.
  • Position via the official transformation grid.
  • Vertical accuracy per BEV ± 0.50 m, see Vertical resolution and accuracy.
  • The DTM shows the terrain without buildings, trees and bridges. For the surface there is the DSM.
  • Coverage is the rectangle 9.53–17.17° E, 46.37–49.02° N. Outside Austria and in gaps the value is a smooth continuation of the terrain, not a measurement.
